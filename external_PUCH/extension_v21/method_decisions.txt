PUCH extension, exploratory revision analysis
21st manuscript version, 24 November 2026

The original PUCH four-gene and IFN-4 results, the original four-cohort panel results, and PUCH cohort-level subtype counts were already known. The new PUCH panel AUCs and transfer results had not been computed when the following choices were recorded. This is a dated analysis note, not a prospectively registered validation study.

1. Use the same 55 PUCH baseline patients, response coding (14 CR/PR versus 41 SD/PD), scale, and per-gene cohort z scoring as the two-score report. Include all five published gene sets. Report all available-gene results regardless of direction and also the fixed original common-gene version. Preserve GEP sign for CD276. Compare standalone fixed-score AUC and rank correlation with IFN-4, with paired, stratified bootstrap uncertainty for AUC differences. Standalone gene weights and score direction are unchanged.
2. For transferable scores choose the archived 733-gene list shared by all five original cohorts, whose 733 symbols, the four IFN-4 symbols and the six common APM symbols are present in the PUCH platform. The broad 15,561-gene set was not fully covered. Choice of this pre-existing universe for assay compatibility used the PUCH gene inventory, not scores, expression distributions or outcomes. Do not replace genes or truncate the universe by cohort. For each sample, give ties their average rank across all 733 genes, center rank percentiles by subtracting 0.5, and average the four or six selected values. Center and scale predictors by pooled training-patient means/sample SD only. Fit two pooled ridge logistic regressions with a single unpenalized intercept and L2 coefficient penalty 1, weighting each of the 249 response-evaluable patients in the four benchmark cohorts equally. Fit both models before opening the PUCH response labels. Perform no recalibration or parameter selection in PUCH. Report model AUC, Brier score and paired differences with external-only, outcome-stratified bootstrap intervals, 5,000 draws; use the same resamples throughout as the original two-score check. All gains are evaluated two-sided. Separate this rank-based model from the within-cohort z-score models and from any trained clinical assay.
3. Treat published aggregate tumor-site counts as describing the analyzed cohort, not as sample-level subtype labels. Do not estimate subtype-specific model performance from aggregate counts.
