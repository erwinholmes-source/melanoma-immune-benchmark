METHODS
Incremental prediction of published gene-set adaptations
We extended the paired incremental analysis to the five published gene-set adaptations (ICR, T-cell-inflamed GEP, chemokine, TLS and APM). This exploratory extension was undertaken during the present revision, after the previous results had been examined. In each of the four cohorts, the baseline model contained IFN-4, and each expanded model added one gene-set score. All comparisons used the same patients, response labels, folds and resampled patient multiplicities as the four-gene analysis. Gene expression was standardized within training folds; the added score was the mean of the available standardized genes, with CD276 negatively signed for GEP, and both scores were then standardized within the training fold. Missing genes were omitted without imputation, preserving the previously reported cohort-specific gene-set adaptations. Gene overlap with IFN-4 was retained and reported; the no-overlap property of the four-gene comparison does not apply to every published set. Ridge logistic regression used a fixed penalty of 1 on the slopes and an unpenalized intercept, without tuning. Predictions were averaged over five repetitions of stratified five-fold cross-validation. Paired changes in AUC and Brier score were calculated on identical patients. Approximate 95% intervals were obtained from 500 outcome-stratified patient bootstrap resamples, each repeating all preprocessing and model fits, with all copies of a resampled patient kept in the same fold. The estimates are conditional on the fixed gene sets and analysis choices; the intervals are exploratory and were not adjusted for the number of gene-set comparisons. Incremental performance was reported separately for each cohort.

RESULTS
Published gene-set adaptations did not show a consistent incremental gain
We directly tested whether the five published gene-set adaptations improved prediction beyond IFN-4. Across the 20 cohort-by-gene-set comparisons, held-out ΔAUC ranged from −0.095 to 0.256, and every approximate 95% interval for ΔAUC and ΔBrier included zero (Table 4; results/increment/table4_incremental_prediction.csv). Adding APM increased AUC from 0.600 to 0.677 in Riaz (ΔAUC 0.077; 95% interval −0.049 to 0.411) and from 0.435 to 0.690 in Hugo (0.256; −0.146 to 0.717), but its increments were −0.022 (−0.126 to 0.242) in Liu and −0.007 (−0.039 to 0.038) in Gide. Thus, APM's high rank against random inflamed gene sets did not establish a consistent conditional improvement over IFN-4: the favorable point estimates in Riaz and Hugo remained imprecise, while little change was observed in Gide. These analyses did not detect a reproducible incremental benefit, but the broad intervals in several cohorts do not exclude substantial gains or losses.

INTERPRETATION
The APM result illustrates why an extreme random-reference percentile and added predictive value are different findings. Direct addition of the APM adaptation to IFN-4 gave favorable but imprecise point estimates in Riaz and Hugo, with no consistent improvement across the four cohorts. This supports further testing of antigen-presentation information under a frozen analysis plan, rather than identifying the present APM adaptation as an established complementary predictor.
