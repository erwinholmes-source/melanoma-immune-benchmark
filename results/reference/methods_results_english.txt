V19 POST HOC SENSITIVITY ANALYSES: REFERENCE BACKGROUND AND SINGLE-SAMPLE RANK SCORING

Methods: reference-background sensitivity
We recovered the complete archived processed expression matrices for the four RNA-seq cohorts and retained the same baseline patients and response endpoint as in the primary analysis. The source matrices contained 22,333 Entrez-indexed rows for Riaz, 25,268 symbol-indexed rows for Hugo, 20,848 for Liu and 22,098 for Gide. Riaz Entrez identifiers were mapped to Approved HGNC symbols using one-to-one symbol–Entrez mappings in the archived HGNC complete-set snapshot downloaded on 7 September 2026. The exact-symbol mappings of the other cohorts were retained, without outcome-dependent substitutions. The broad reference background comprised 15,561 HGNC protein-coding genes that were uniquely measured, finite and nonconstant across baseline patients in every RNA-seq cohort. This is a common measured protein-coding background, not the entire human transcriptome. Expression values were checked against the corresponding compact archived inputs, and original four-gene scores and previously reported scalar AUCs were reproduced.

We compared the four-gene score with 10,000 random four-gene sets from each of three nested backgrounds: the 15,561 common protein-coding genes, the historical 733 genes shared across the four RNA-seq cohorts and the NanoString cohort, and the historical subset of 142 genes correlated with CD8A at Spearman rho >= 0.5 in each of those five cohorts. All three backgrounds contained the four panel genes. Gene eligibility used expression and annotation without response labels. Each random set contained four distinct genes; sets were drawn independently, allowing a set to recur between draws. The same set was applied to all four evaluation cohorts. Scores were equal-weight means of within-cohort baseline-standardized expression, with sample standard deviations and fixed positive directions. The primary random-reference statistic was the equally weighted mean of the four cohort-specific AUCs. Ties were assigned one half. We reported the observed score's midrank percentile and nominal upper-tail Monte Carlo P value, (1 + number of random statistics at least as large as observed)/10,001. The historical seeds (20261102 and 20261103) were retained for the 733- and 142-gene references; the broader reference used seed 20261123. The 100,000 archived random AUC values across the two historical backgrounds, four cohorts and four-cohort means were reproduced exactly. These analyses were added post hoc; the conditional CD8A reference was not treated as independent validation or as a genome-wide null.

Methods: single-sample rank sensitivity
To assess dependence on the within-cohort z-score rule, we ranked the same 15,561 genes within each baseline sample, assigning average ranks to ties. Each gene's centered percentile was (rank - 1)/(15,561 - 1) - 0.5. A panel's rank score was the mean signed percentile across a fixed subset of its genes present in the common background. The four-gene score and IFN-4 retained all four genes. The fixed ICR, GEP, chemokine, TLS and APM adaptations contained 19, 17, 8, 8 and 6 genes, respectively. The exclusions were CCL5 from ICR and GEP; CCL3, CCL4, CCL5 and CCL18 from the chemokine set; CCR6 from TLS; and TAP2 from APM. CD276 retained its negative sign in GEP; all other genes were positively signed. Missing genes were not imputed. For a like-for-like comparison, the same common gene subsets were also scored by the original equal-weight z-score rule; available-gene z-score adaptations were retained separately to distinguish gene-coverage changes from scoring-rule changes.

We calculated within-cohort Spearman correlations across all baseline records and AUCs in response-evaluable patients. Paired 95% percentile intervals for rank-minus-z AUC differences used 5,000 outcome-stratified patient bootstrap resamples, with identical patient weights for the two scoring rules. These intervals are conditional on the fixed background, gene membership and constructed scores; they are not intervals for a learned prediction algorithm. Source transformations were retained: Riaz used the authors' transformed matrix, Hugo log2(FPKM + 1), and Liu and Gide log2(TPM + 1). Consequently, this analysis tests a scoring-rule sensitivity in the available processed data and does not establish assay-level transportability or reproduce the original published algorithms and weights.

Results: dependence on the reference background
The four RNA-seq cohorts contributed 253 baseline records and 249 response-evaluable patients (Riaz 49, Hugo 26, Liu 102 and Gide 72). The four-gene score's mean cohort AUC was 0.621. Random sets from the 15,561-gene protein-coding background had a median mean-cohort AUC of 0.490 (interquartile range 0.457–0.523), placing the score at the 99.72nd percentile (nominal P = 0.00290). In the historical 733-gene background, the median increased to 0.548 (0.513–0.579), with the score at the 96.32nd percentile (P = 0.03690). Conditioning further on the 142 CD8A-correlated genes increased the median to 0.607 (0.597–0.616), placing the score at the 86.95th percentile (P = 0.13059). Thus, the score's apparent advantage depended on the biological composition of the reference background: it exceeded most broad protein-coding combinations, while much of that advantage was shared by combinations of inflammation-associated genes. This result does not establish that all random gene sets perform similarly or that the four-gene score lacks incremental prediction information.

Results: rank-scoring sensitivity
Within-sample rank scores remained strongly correlated with their common-gene z-score counterparts: Spearman rho ranged from 0.934 to 0.974 for the four-gene score and from 0.945 to 0.991 for IFN-4. The rank-based four-gene score also remained strongly correlated with rank-based IFN-4 (rho 0.906–0.942). Its cohort-specific AUCs were 0.638 in Riaz, 0.500 in Hugo, 0.547 in Liu and 0.827 in Gide, compared with 0.621, 0.476, 0.553 and 0.835 under the z-score rule. IFN-4 rank AUCs were 0.651, 0.446, 0.525 and 0.834, respectively. Rank-minus-z AUC changes were -0.009 to 0.024 for the four-gene score and -0.015 to 0.000 for IFN-4; their paired intervals all included zero. Across the seven fixed gene-set adaptations and four cohorts, all 28 paired intervals included zero, although some allowed appreciable changes and therefore do not establish equivalence.

The common-membership rank adaptations had mean cohort AUCs of 0.621 for ICR, 0.627 for GEP, 0.611 for the chemokine set, 0.614 for TLS and 0.638 for APM. ICR and GEP retained strong rank-score correlations with IFN-4 (rho 0.931–0.964 and 0.898–0.964, respectively). The cohort pattern remained evident: AUCs of the four-gene, IFN-4, ICR, GEP and chemokine rank scores ranged from 0.817 to 0.839 in Gide, versus 0.440–0.500 in Hugo and 0.521–0.558 in Liu. APM remained a distinct exploratory observation in Hugo (rank AUC 0.702), but this is a single-set discrimination result rather than an incremental-value test.

Interpretation for the manuscript
These results support two bounded statements: (1) the four-gene panel's advantage over a broad gene background is substantially attenuated when the comparator is restricted to the same inflammation-associated expression program; (2) the close correspondence between the four-gene score and an IFN-4 index does not arise solely from the within-cohort z-score construction. They do not replace external validation, resolve between-cohort heterogeneity, or prove that all immune signatures are equivalent. The reference backgrounds were defined using the current study cohorts' expression distributions, although not their outcomes. Both backgrounds and signatures should be frozen before future external evaluation.
